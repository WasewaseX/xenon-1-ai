# Chapter 4 — The Silent Chorus

The Chorus hired desperate cutters, and Dar was nothing if not desperate. He walked through the Choratorium's bone-arched entrance, his borrowed Chorus white gloves pristine, his pulse steady beneath the borrowed coat. The air here smelled of antiseptic and something else—copper and honey, like blood preserved in sugar. A Clinic Custodian with bone-plating across her cheeks greeted him, her voice a smooth, practiced melody. "Welcome, cutter. We've been expecting you. The harvest floor awaits your skilled hands."

The harvest floor was a cathedral of glass and brass, a forest of vitrines where debtors floated in nutrient fluid, their chests opened like books. Some had multiple hearts beating in tandem, others had lungs grown in pairs where one should be, all wired to monitors that tracked their vital signs with the precision of a metronome. Nurses moved between the vitrines with clipboards, making notations as if they were tallying inventory.

"Each vessel is a note in our grand composition," the Custodian explained, leading Dar past a man with three livers, each a different shade of crimson. "By isolating the organs, we allow each to sing its purest song. Together, they create a harmony no single body could achieve."

Dar nodded, his gaze fixed on a ledger wall where census pages were filed alphabetically. His own page would be there, the one that flagged him as a missing vessel. He needed to find it, to steal it, to erase the record that would lead the Chorus to him.

He feigned interest in a nearby vitrine, a woman with her nervous system mapped across her torso like a circuit board. "Fascinating," he murmured, his voice tight. "The precision required..."

"The human body is the most intricate instrument," the Custodian agreed. "We are merely the conductors, ensuring each note is played to its fullest potential."

Dar's fingers brushed against the ledger wall, his heart pounding beneath the Lily's steady rhythm. He found the 'M' section and began flipping through the pages, his breath held. There—his census page. He slipped it into his coat, his movements practiced, casual.

But then something else caught his eye—the Choratorium nursery ledger. Entries listed vessels like produce, graded on a scale of A to F. One entry stood out: ILAY, PERFECT, RESERVED FOR THE THRONE. Dar's blood ran cold. This was the child Suri had mentioned, the one who had escaped.

"Lost in the music, cutter?" The Custodian's voice was closer than he'd expected. Dar turned to find her standing beside him, her bone-plated face unreadable. "We keep meticulous records. Every vessel, every note, accounted for."

"Merely admiring your system," Dar said, forcing a smile. "The efficiency is... admirable."

The Custodian's gaze lingered on him, her head tilted slightly. "You carry the scent of something else. Something... alive. Not of the Chorus."

Dar's heart hammered against his ribs. The Lily inside him pulsed, a silent warning. "I—I don't know what you mean," he stammered, taking a step back.

The Custodian's smile didn't reach her eyes. "Of course you don't. But we do." She raised a hand, and two Choirmasters in bone masks stepped forward, their scalpel-tipped fingers glinting in the harsh light. "Perhaps we should examine you more closely."

Dar's mind raced. He needed to stall, to buy time. "The harvest floor," he said, gesturing vaguely. "The arrangements here... they're not optimal. The fluid ratios are off by 2.3 percent. It's affecting the organ viability."

The Choirmasters froze, their scalpel-fingers still. The Custodian's expression shifted, curiosity replacing suspicion. "Is that so? Explain."

Dar launched into a detailed explanation of fluid dynamics, his voice steady despite the terror coursing through him. He spoke of osmotic pressure, of nutrient absorption rates, of how even a slight imbalance could compromise the viability of harvested organs. The Custodian listened, her head tilted, her expression thoughtful.

"An interesting observation," she said finally. "But we've been using these ratios for years."

"Times change," Dar said, his pulse hammering in his throat. "New research, new techniques. I could show you. Adjust the ratios myself. It would only take a moment."

The Custodian considered this, then nodded. "Very well. Show us. But make it quick. The choir awaits its conductor."

Dar led them to a nearby vitrine, his hands trembling as he pretended to adjust the fluid ratios. The Choirmasters stood watch, their scalpel-fingers poised. Dar's eyes darted around, searching for an escape route. The corridor behind him was lined with harvested organs, preserved in jars and hanging from hooks.

"Is that it?" the Custodian asked, her voice impatient.

"Almost," Dar said, his gaze falling on a row of hanging lungs. "The ventilation system needs recalibration. It's affecting the oxygenation levels."

The Custodian sighed. "Fine. Show us."

As she moved closer, Dar made his move. He grabbed a nearby jar of preserved kidneys and hurled it at the Choirmasters. As they ducked, Dar bolted, running down the corridor, his heart pounding against his ribs. The Lily inside him pulsed, urging him faster.

He ducked into a side corridor, the sound of footsteps echoing behind him. He ran past rows of harvested hearts, livers, lungs, each one a silent testament to the Chorus's methodology. The air grew colder, the scent of antiseptic giving way to something else—something ancient and hungry.

Dar reached a heavy metal door and pushed through it, stumbling into a dimly lit chamber. The walls were lined with shelves, each holding a different organ, each one still alive, still singing its silent song. In the center of the room stood a massive glass tank, filled with a viscous, amber fluid. Inside, something moved—something vast and slow, its form shifting in the depths.

Dar froze, his breath catching in his throat. The Lily inside him pulsed, a strange recognition washing over him. This was the First Vessel, the buried proto-Throne the Chorus was trying to awaken.

"Found you," a voice said from behind him.

Dar turned to see the Custodian standing in the doorway, a bone scalpel in her hand. "You shouldn't be here," she said, her voice calm. "This is sacred ground."

Dar backed away, his hand on the door handle. "I'm leaving," he said, his voice tight.

The Custodian took a step forward. "You carry something that doesn't belong to you. Something that was planted with love."

Dar hesitated, then turned and fled, pushing through the door and back into the corridor. He ran, his lungs burning, the Lily urging him onward. He reached the entrance, bursting through the bone-arched doors and into the harsh light of the Choratorium plaza.

As he stumbled out, every ceiling speaker in the clinic crackled to life. A woman's voice, calm and kind, filled the air.

"To whoever carries our missing garden home," the voice said. "She was planted with love. Bring her back before she roots in strangers."

Dar froze, the voice echoing in his mind. The Lily inside him shivered—whether with fear or longing, he couldn't tell.
