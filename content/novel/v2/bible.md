## 1. Premise & Tone

In the salt-strangled city of **Ashkarad**, the body is currency. A failing heart is replaced with a **gravel-heart** bought on seven years of indenture to **Saint Ferrous Hospital**; skin is sold for carbon grafts; organs are tithed against debts that grow instead of shrink. Everything is written down — census ledgers, vital-debt columns, death notices — and everything is counted. The ruling **Church of the Silent Chorus** runs the tithe-system through its cathedral-clinic, the **Choratorium**, harvesting debtors' organs into glass reliquaries where they keep singing as **choir organs**. The salt storms that white out the city for days are called **"grace"** by everyone who doesn't have to breathe them.

Darius "Dar" Mehran, a debt-bound surgeon, steals a Church heart and is instead chosen by it: the **Umbra Lily**, a black-petaled symbiote that roots in his ruined chest, gives him a second heartbeat, and begins opening the **Nine Veins** — a biological ladder of transcendence from Flesh to Throne. Each Vein is power with a price: blood, sensory overload, **memory bleed**, and finally **bloom-through**, where the carrier stops being a person and becomes a garden. Dar's war is against the Chorus's Custodian **Anra Vey**, who spent forty years building the **First Vessel** (the **First Singer**) beneath the Marrowworks and meant to become the city's heart during the **Blacktide** — the salt-moon eclipse.

The novel's deep game is bigger than Vey. The Lily is the ninth of nine sibling seeds sown a century ago and signed **THE GARDENER**; eight cities already bloomed **TRUE THRONE**. Ashkarad's page reads **VESSEL: FLED—RECLAIM**. The epilogue ends the existing material on the cliffhanger: a vast, patient second heartbeat under the earth, and the Lily's first spoken word — **"Sister."**

Tone: dark biological fantasy (Shadow Slave × Lord of the Mysteries × biopunk horror) with a working-class accounting register. Horror is administrative before it is carnal — the scariest thing in Ashkarad is a form stamped correctly. Intimacy is priced, kindness is an unpriced anomaly, and hope is a ledger entry someone keeps counting anyway.

## 2. Voice & Style Guide

**POV & tense.**
- Default: third person limited, past tense, locked to Dar. Other limited POVs are allowed only in interludes (Interlude I = Milla Ferun; Interlude II = Suri; Interlude III = the Lily, in *second person present tense* — "You are not planted in soil but in salt…"). The epilogue opens in present tense ("The silence in Ashkarad is louder than the song ever was") then settles into past — treat that as coda flavor, not a license.
- DO NOT slip into first person inside a third-person chapter. (ch13 does this by accident — "inside my skull", "I couldn't answer" — it is a known defect, not a style.)
- Scene breaks are a line of `---` or `· · ·`. Chapters run ~900–1,600 words (interlude-3 runs long by design).

**Sentence rhythm.**
- Mid-length to long compound sentences built on em-dashes and clinical lists; counts embedded in narration ("Sixty-three steps to the first drop"; "Twelve thousand, seven hundred and forty-three"). Dialogue is short, transactional, and dry — people talk like counterparties closing a deal. Gallows humor from Dar; flat, counted speech from Suri; slurred-then-laser-sharp lectures from Vahid; warm, maternal menace from Vey.
- Physics of horror: resonance is felt **in the teeth** ("a sound… that made Dar's teeth ache"), silence is described as pressure, darkness as presence ("not a shade but a presence").

**The debt / ledger / accounting metaphor system (the novel's signature).** ALL biology and power is written as finance. Use it constantly and precisely:
- Body as ledger: "his body was a ledger of IOUs" (prologue); Vahid: "The body is a ledger, and every entry has a cost" (epilogue).
- Debt mechanics: vital-debt, tithes, interest compounding "every salt-moon", grace periods, audits, death notices, receipts, resold debts, "UNKNOWN LIABILITY" columns.
- Grafts as receipts: Suri's carbon scales are "a receipt"; the Lily's root-system is drawn as accounts ("filing something away in whatever ledger it kept inside him").
- Vein-opening as spending: at the Mind Vein, memories are "spent, paid to the darkness to keep himself whole… spent like coin in the darkness."
- Emotions priced: "The first unpriced thing anyone had ever given her" (Suri on Dar's stitching); "DAR MEHRAN — PAID IN FULL (keep counting anyway)".
- The Lily's cravings are invoiced: blood given in the dark, light as a cost, darkness as credit.

**Motif list (weave, don't stack):**
1. **Salt** — storms called "grace"; the **salt-moon**; **Blacktide** (the eclipse); salt gardens; dawn "bleached white"; a wind that "tasted of iron and salt".
2. **Song / singing** — choir organs humming in the walls; the canal-singer (Lena Vance); Ilay hearing "the singing in the walls"; hymns taught to nursery children "to make their tissue compliant"; the lullaby; "the city's song"; after the fall, "the silence is loud."
3. **Heartbeat** — Dar's two heartbeats; pulse-readers; "DO NOT COUNT THE HEARTBEATS"; the heartbeats in the deep place "count back"; heartbeats as lanterns in the Lily's dream.
4. **Ledgers** — census pages, stamps (Heart-Stamp, Debt-Stamp, Voluntary-Stamp), the World Registry, burning ledgers, chalk flower census marks.
5. **Masks** — bone-plate masks "smooth as skulls"; the Cartographer's brass mask; white Chorus masks; the blank porcelain face in Milla's dream; Roya wearing the brass mask "always now".
6. **Gardens** — the salt garden with sleepers "planted… like bulbs"; pruning, tending, bloom-through; black lace / black vascular lace petals; frost petals on syndicate banners all pointing "compass-true, toward the Choratorium"; the registry's flower symbol "blooming from a crack in the earth."
7. Secondary: white gloves (Vey, the Gardener's hands); tea as ritual/threat ("decide which cup to drink from"); the five-petaled flower; glass/reliquaries; **heat-berries** (bioluminescent fungi); counting itself.

**What to avoid.**
- No first-person slips in third-person chapters; no "Breath Veil" typo (epilogue's error — it's the Breath **Vein**).
- **Blacktide is an event** (the salt-moon eclipse), not a district. Do not use it as a place name.
- The organs are "**choir organs**" (also "chorus" appears once, ch13, as "a chorus of screams" — different sense). Don't coin "chorus organs".
- The Lily almost never speaks. Total spoken lines in the entire book: "You are mine now." (prologue), "Then carry me a little further." (twice, ch17), "Sister." (epilogue). Keep it to gesture, pulse, and craving; speech must be an event.
- Don't casually restore Dar's spent memories, resurrect Vey, or explain the Gardener outright. Costs already paid stay paid.
- No modern tech slang, real-world brands, or Earth geography. Tech is brass, bone, glass, hand-cranked, biogel, wax cylinders.
- Avoid melodrama and revenge clichés; conflict resolves through negotiation, triage, and price, with violence as surgery ("He wasn't fighting them; he was redirecting them").
- Don't add new named districts that contradict the map below.

## 3. World & System

### The city — Ashkarad
A vertical salt-port under storm-walls; glass funiculars carry the rich above permanent fog; the poor live below. Established places (exact names):
- **Saltstairs** — Dar's home district; "a stacked canyon of crumbling brick and rusted iron"; tenements, cisterns beneath it, bolt-holes, syndicate banners.
- **Marrowworks** — the organ industry: organ docks, tithe-warehouses, harvest floors, tithe-clinics, bioluminescent signs; the deep shaft to the First Singer is "beneath the Marrowworks."
- **The Undergleam** — black-market warren of tunnels and stalls beneath the streets (information, contraband organs, "jars of preserved fears… forgotten dreams"); the Cartographer's territory ("doesn't deal in contraband—only maps"); drainage canals.
- **The Choratorium** — the Church's bone-arched cathedral-clinic: harvest floor, reliquary archive, nursery wing, graft-theater, Vey's dining hall, her private sanctum; "the reliquaries where they keep the harvested voices."
- **Saint Ferrous Hospital** — Dar's employer/debt-holder.
- Also: the **storm-walls**, **seawalls** (debt-prison "in the seawalls"), canal-works and pump vanes, abandoned cisterns, Mother Gavel's **fighting pit** (the quarry), the eastern tea slopes (now blighted grey).

### The Choir, chorus organs, and debt-tracking
The **Church of the Silent Chorus** (a.k.a. the Chorus, the Church) administers the city through **vital-debt**: treatment or survival is granted against years, blood, and organs; debts grow with interest and are inherited/resold. Instruments of the system: census ledgers and census chits (brass discs), **pulse-readers** (hand-cranked devices that read rhythms and print slips — one of Dar's read **ERROR: RHYTHM UNRESOLVED. MACHINE FAULT.**), tithe-clerks with the Heart-Stamp / Debt-Stamp / Voluntary-Stamp, "Voluntary" consent forms, tithe-barges, **bone-plate enforcers** with pulse-readers scanning for heartbeats, and the **choir organs** — forty years of harvested, still-living organs in glass that hum through the Choratorium's walls and, crucially, **track debtors by their unique biological signatures** (epilogue). A chalk **five-petaled flower** on a doorframe is a census mark ("A census. A count. And one vessel was missing."). The **nursery** raises children as vessels, graded A–F ("ILAY, PERFECT, RESERVED FOR THE THRONE"), taught hymns so their tissue accepts grafts. Since the First Singer's death-song silenced every choir organ, "the Wardens' pulse-readers were just machines counting heartbeats" — the system is fraying and **the ledgers are burning**.

Power outside the Church: the syndicates — **the Gavel** (boss: **Mother Gavel**; ledgermen, enforcers, fighting pit) and **the Hammers** (rival) — plus the **Syndic Wardens** (grey coats, hand-cranked pulse-readers, curfew bells) and informants called **ledgermen**. After ch15's **Truce of Salt**: no tithe-taking from the Saltstairs, no harvesting, no debtors to the ledgers without trial.

### The Nine Veins — body-cultivation ladder
Named in ch05 by Vahid: "Flesh, Marrow, Blood, Breath, Nerve, Bone, Mind, Soul, and… Throne." Ch01 gives the doctrine form: "First Vein: Flesh. Wounds knit, strength swells."

**Standing costs of the ladder (ch05, binding):** feeding requirements (the Lily drinks blood, prefers it given in darkness), sensory overload, **memory bleed** (echoes of other people's lived moments), and **BLOOM-THROUGH** — "The Lily doesn't want to live in you. It wants to become through you. Most carriers die of the process. The rest… stop being people. They become gardens." Forcing a bloom back leaves temporary numbness (ch09). The Cartographer's warning (ch09): "Every bloom is a door, cutter. Something on the far side has started counting your heartbeats." (It stood at 109.) Rule pair to keep consistent: **darkness feeds the Lily, light weakens it; blood craved when Dar bleeds.**

**Dar's progress:** at story end he is at **Vein Seven — Mind** (stated twice: ch18, epilogue). He has opened Veins One through Seven; Soul and Throne lie ahead.

### The Umbra Lily
- Nature: a symbiotic organism, "a black umbra lily, its petals not of flesh but of shadow, wrapped around a central core that pulsed with a dim, violet light." It chose Dar when the stolen reliquary cracked: it replaced his failing gravel-heart ("Your heart is gone… Replaced by something that wanted the position" — Vahid).
- Origin: Vahid and his partner **Dr. Shirin Bahar** built the ancestor strain — "a heart that couldn't break… It wasn't a heart. It was a seed. And seeds are patient." The Church has "been refining the process ever since." Vahid insists it is "ancient. Older than the Church. Older than Ashkarad itself."
- Behavior: darkness feeds it, light hurts it; drinks blood given in the dark; craves blood viciously when Dar bleeds; sings (a hum Dar feels in his bones); dreams — the **salt garden**, rows of sleepers "planted… like bulbs," a tower "each window a heartbeat," and a humming woman; it bleeds memories into Dar (the woman in white gloves tending the salt garden, humming a lullaby in a dead language).
- Relationship arc: threat → guest → "her." Dar "called it 'her' now, though he never said the word aloud. It felt more respectful." It yielded to his refusal at the Blacktide ("Then carry me a little further.") and finally spoke in the epilogue: **"Sister."**
- Interlude III (the Lily's own dream, second person): it is the **ninth of nine** lilies formed in the dark; eight siblings carried off in lead-and-glass reliquaries "to find your hearts"; a voice commands "Go. Find your hearts. And bloom." Its carrier boy fell ("a dying lantern in the fog"); it passed hand to hand and came to rest in formaldehyde; it absorbed its host's memories when it fused ("You feel his memories flooding into you"). **Implication on file: the Lily may hold copies of the memories Dar spent — including his mother's face.**

### The First Vessel / First Singer
A buried proto-Throne: "a single shaft, deep beneath the Marrowworks, older than the Church above it" (Cartographer's map; its margin warns **DO NOT COUNT THE HEARTBEATS** — "Because it counts back… The heartbeats in that place aren't just alive—they're aware. And they remember."). Forty years of harvested organs grafted onto one dreaming host: "a cathedral of flesh grown too large for its space… its head a grotesque mockery of a face with too many eyes, all closed." Vey: "It will not wake. It dreams, but it cannot dream itself whole… The First Vessel is locked from the outside. It needs a living Throne to open the door from inside." During the Blacktide it opened one vast eye, woke, and released a song "not of joy but of release" that freed every harvested donor and silenced all choir organs citywide (ch18). The epilogue calls it "the dead First Vessel." In ch13 it answered Dar's heartbeat with his mother's lullaby.

### Vey, the World Registry, and THE GARDENER
**Custodian Anra Vey** led the Church, kept a private salt-garden sanctum beneath the Choratorium, and orchestrated the Blacktide ascension. She dissolved into black lace petals during the ritual; only her white gloves remained (ch18). In her sanctum lay the **World Registry**: "Nine entries, nine cities, nine seeds sown a century ago by a hand signed only 'THE GARDENER.' Eight entries were stamped **BLOOMED—TRUE THRONE**. Ashkarad's read: **VESSEL: FLED—RECLAIM**." Dar keeps Vey's registry page (epilogue): "It listed eight cities, all marked with the same symbol: a stylized flower blooming from a crack in the earth. One city was circled in red ink: Ashkarad. Below the list, a single word: **RECLAIM**. And a title: **GARDENER**." The Gardener appears in the Lily's dream as a woman, "her face a mask of calm, her hands white as bone," humming the lullaby; she sent the nine seeds out with "Go. Find your hearts. And bloom." Identity unconfirmed — the text deliberately withholds whether the Gardener is Vey, Vey's master, or something older.

## 4. Character Sheet

**Darius "Dar" Mehran** — protagonist; debt-bound surgical intern at Saint Ferrous Hospital, 27; heart failed at nineteen, replaced by a **gravel-heart** bought with seven years of owed labor. Physical: gut-shot healed by the Lily in the prologue (scar "sealed… leaving only the memory of pain"); faint silvery veins over his chest; black vascular lace blooms on his skin under power; now broad-shouldered, dense-boned, unreadably steady. The Mind Vein's price: he has **spent** his mother's face, his father's laugh, his first kiss, his childhood home's color. Speech: dry, precise, surgeon's triage cadence, gallows humor ("Death's a paperwork problem. They haven't filed it yet."); refuses transactions he can't stand behind ("It's a guest. And guests don't get to choose the house for their host."). Relationships: Suri (patient he stitched, then partner — the person he refuses to price), Ilay (protective hope; "This was hope. Fragile, dangerous hope"), Vahid (mentor), Roya (ally/mapmaker), Vey (antagonist who offered him his regrown original heart). Arc status: refused the Throne twice, broke the Blacktide ritual, survived with the Lily willingly aboard; runs a free clinic in the Saltstairs; at Vein Seven; heard the second heartbeat and the word "Sister."

**Suri Kaveh** — debt-courier, fixer, the novel's counter. Physical: right arm sheathed in a **carbon-scale** graft (sold her skin at nineteen to cover her mother's surgery; kept six weeks; ransomed back when her unnamed brother found coin); hairline fracture in that ulna after ch12; the graft was **split and burned, ruined** in ch18; left forearm splinted (new crude splint in the epilogue); tiny scars around her eyes from traded skin. Speech: flat, economical, always counting aloud — steps, tiles, heartbeats, lies; ledger metaphors ("That's a ledger imbalance, Mehran"). Relationships: Dar — the unpriced debt she finally filed as "DAR MEHRAN — PAID IN FULL (keep counting anyway)"; Ilay ("little thistle"); Mother Gavel demanded her as a counter of enemies — Dar refused: "She works for me." Arc status: free of the Gavel by truce, maimed but operating, auditing a new "CHORUS — UNKNOWN LIABILITY" column; her name may sit in the Church's vessel registry (her graft carries a registry number; the debt was "resold three times").

**Professor Vahid Anousheh** — disgraced anatomist, "The Professor of Impossible Anatomy"; accreditation taken by the Syndic Wardens; exile den in a salt-rusted anatomy theater; gin-soaked but exact. Co-created the Lily's ancestor strain with **Dr. Shirin Bahar**; his discredited papers are the Nine Veins' only map ("It is not evil. It is alien. It wants to bloom. And a bloom has a direction."). He taught Dar every Vein's key (control / grounding / will). Went alone to the Choratorium to take back Shirin's kept voice — "They kept her voice six years. I am going to take it back. Forgive the plagiarism — I always stole your best lines." — and was taken (ch16: strapped to the altar as "the keystone"). Physical: left arm lost — ch18 shows it as living Chorus tissue; the epilogue shows him with "his one remaining hand… his stump" (report the arm as amputated; the cause differs between files, see §7). Speech: lectures that swerve from drunken rambling to surgical clarity; calls Dar "boy." Arc status: survived, teaching at Dar's clinic, "his lectures growing more fervent"; keeper of the Lily's secrets.

**Roya — the Cartographer** — info-broker of the Undergleam; deals only in maps; brass mask covering forehead to chin; maps "with pressure… lines rising from the chalkboard like veins beneath skin"; wired a **song-lobe** to Dar's temples to map the Lily's dream. Ch18 unmasks her: "revealing Roya beneath the brass mask, her face streaked with grime and tears." Physical: song-lobe connection to the city's living tissue **severed** by the First Singer's song; a permanent white blade-scar on her hand; wears the brass mask "always now." Speech: metallic calm, short declaratives ("Every bloom is a door, cutter."; "I call it architecture."). Relationships: broker-ally to Dar and Suri; hunted once by Vey ("The one who's been hunting my signal too"); guards Ilay ("It's different now… The silence is loud."). Arc status: powerless but present, co-guardian of Ilay; her past and her mask's reason are unexplored.

**Ilay** — nine-year-old girl; Church registry entry "VESSEL: ILAY / AGE: 9 / GRADE: PERFECT / RESERVED: THRONE" with the column **SIBLING STOCK: ACTIVE**. Called **"the garden's daughter"** by the singing. Physical: small, quiet, unnerving stillness; eyes "the color of river stones"; hair described "spun copper" in ch11 but "dark" in ch12 (see §7). She hears the singing in the walls, mapped the shaft to the First Singer **by ear** ("The numbers are how many steps down. The circles are how loud it gets."), and post-collapse still hears "a music only she could… hear," drawing "where the singing used to be." Speech: sparse, eerie, literal. Relationships: Dar (rescuer/anchor), Suri (guarded her), Roya (companion), Vey (wanted her as "the perfect bloom… With her, the Ninth Vein is possible"). Arc status: rescued from the nursery raid, living at the clinic, drawing silence-swirls; unawakened key.

**Custodian Anra Vey** — primary antagonist (deceased). Leader of the Church of the Silent Chorus; serene middle-aged beauty, "her hair the color of salt and ash," white gloves, bone-plate mask in public. Speech: warm, maternal, absolute — tea rituals, endearments that are threats ("Bring her back before she roots in strangers."; "You just have to decide which cup to drink from."). Believed Ashkarad "is a body that refuses to be whole… And I will be its heart." Offered Dar his original heart (regrown, "beats in glass between the candles in the dining hall") in exchange for Ilay. Arc status: began bloom-through at the Blacktide, was unmade by the First Singer's release-song — "Her form dissolved into black lace… Only her white gloves remained on the altar rail." **Dr. Shirin Bahar** — Vahid's partner; co-creator of the Lily ancestor strain; harvested by the Church; "They kept her voice six years"; her voice sang in the Choratorium (wax cylinder; a preserved brain-lobe in the reliquary, which Vahid smashed in grief — "I couldn't save her… But I could free the others"). ## 5. Chronological Event Timeline (story order)

Reading/story order per the established manifest: 00-prologue → ch01–ch06 → interlude-1 → ch07–ch12 → interlude-2 → ch13–ch16 → interlude-3 → ch17 → ch18 → 19-epilogue.

1. **00-prologue.md — "The Last Honest Thief"** (story day 0). During a salt storm called grace, Dar's crew hits the Marrowworks organ docks for a Church heart. Kael runs cover; Suri counts patrols; bone-plate enforcers massacre the crew; Dar is gut-shot; the reliquary cracks and the black lily unfolds toward him. He wakes healed, two-hearted, with a voice in his mind: "You are mine now." An unnamed enforcer captain tastes his blood: "It chose… The choir must be complete."
2. **ch01.md — "Two Heartbeats"** (day 2). Dar takes stock in his Saltstairs cot: silvery veins, dark/light rule, blood-craving rule. He studies Vahid's discredited Nine Veins papers. Suri gives him a stolen pulse-reader to practice for a Warden audit; the machine prints ERROR: RHYTHM UNRESOLVED. MACHINE FAULT. A chalk five-petal flower census mark appears across the alley — "one vessel was missing."
3. **ch02.md — "The Courier"**. Suri confronts Dar (she heard two hearts), trades a favor: file his death notice as natural causes; he runs her package through the Undergleam. At the drainage canal the wall blooms with black petals — the harvested courier **Lena Vance** sings from inside the stone, and the Lily sings back.
4. **ch03.md — "Grace"**. Sheltering in a cellar during a storm, in total darkness, Dar cuts his own palm rather than take Suri's blood. The Lily drinks; **the Marrow Vein opens** (dense bones, expanded senses). At dawn, frost on the wall has bloomed into petals reaching toward where Suri slept — the Lily nearly chose her.
5. **ch04.md — "The Silent Chorus"**. Dar infiltrates the Choratorium as a hired cutter; sees the harvest floor (debtors as "vessels," organs kept alive in glass), steals his own census page, finds the nursery ledger entry "ILAY, PERFECT, RESERVED FOR THE THRONE," stumbles on the chamber of the **First Vessel**, is nearly caught by the Clinic Custodian, and escapes as Vey's voice broadcasts citywide: "Bring her back before she roots in strangers."
6. **ch05.md — "The Professor of Impossible Anatomy"**. Vahid examines Dar: the Lily is "a key"; he chalks the Nine Veins ladder, names the costs (feeding, sensory overload, memory bleed, BLOOM-THROUGH), shows photographs of his and Shirin Bahar's first volunteer blooming into a garden, and plays her preserved voice. Plan: find the other carriers, stop Vey before the Blacktide.
