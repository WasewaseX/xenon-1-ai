# Summaries — Chapters 24–29 (ARC II: The Second Heartbeat)

## Chapter 24 — The Silence Audit
Three weeks after the Blacktide, Ashkarad counts its dead by hand (412 names chalked on the cistern wall) while Dar's free clinic overflows with 31 patients. Warden-Auditor Seft arrives with a newly built pulse-reader demanding the clinic's patient ledger "for reconciliation"; Dar refuses to surrender names and posts the audit's own terms on the clinic door, unpriced — the refusal spreads as a count of doorways that refuse. Before leaving, Seft runs a "sample reconciliation" and his new machine flags three stock children (Elias among them) with RHYTHM UNRESOLVED, then prints unprompted: REQ: VESSEL REGISTRY — PAGE NINE. Dar keeps the three confiscated slips beside Vey's registry page; the hook is that someone in the rebuilt Warden apparatus is keeping a vessel registry counted by pages, and it is missing one.

## Chapter 25 — What the Fire Kept
The Wardens' census halls burn in one night — debtor fires, clerk fires destroying evidence, and three "organized" fires laid by a grey-coat hardliner faction that names itself the Index (blank-rectangle tar armband; creed: burn what can't be audited, count every soul once under one seal). Suri leads six couriers (including a young courier, Nim) into the records annex to salvage name-pages, rescuing dockets including Kaelen's BLOOMED THROUGH page and a nursery intake sheet listing the eleven vessels; she skirmishes the Index non-lethally via a brine-tank drop. In the ash she finds her own registry page — name, graft-number, RESOLD — THREE TIMES — and files it in her own ledger's spine (it survives for later arcs). Hook: a salvage clerk whispers the Index is posting rising bounties for "the Ferun printout"; Suri records FERUN — ? and "(ask Dar)".

## Chapter 26 — The Unspent Room
Vahid (one-handed, wire-mended spectacles, chalk ladder of the Nine Veins in the clinic's cold cellar) begins Dar's Mind Vein training: memory as architecture — walk the corridors without withdrawing; swept rooms (nail-holes remain) versus never-built rooms; grief is a withdrawal. Across nine walks the discipline holds (mother's face, father's laugh, first kiss, home's color inventoried without payment, headaches as the "teller's fee"). After the ninth session Dar returns alone and finds, at the end of his deepest hall, a furnished door he never built — wooden frame, lamplight in the hairline, humming a tune he almost knows, warm on the far side like a room someone has just left. He refuses the handle; Vahid boxes the law on the wall: WE DO NOT OPEN DOORS WE DID NOT BUILD, calling the door a "tenancy" — hook: the warmth of recent presence, and the Lily settling deeper "into a room it liked."

## Chapter 27 — Where the Singing Went
Roya (song-lobe severed, brass mask permanent) begins mapping through Ilay as her living instrument: the girl's silence-drawings have changed from "where the singing used to be" to something that moves — the deep heartbeat walking a slow spiral of tightening, dated rings. The new wall-map converges under the Marrowworks, under the Throne shaft, matching Dar's rooftop hearings night for night; Ilay reports the big sound walks in smaller circles and, underneath it, a small one "keeps time with Dar" — it is lonely, and coming to the only other one. The hook: the spiral's innermost (fifteenth) ring is dated in Ilay's hand with one word — "soon" — and Roya annotates the chart "datum confirmed. not for sale."

## Chapter 28 — Sanctuary Terms
Mother Gavel annexes the Choratorium's ruins (seven posted clauses; "protection dues" payable in coin, labor, or records) and posts a rider naming the clinic's patient registry the price of protection as the Truce of Salt frays. When four Gavel enforcers and a ledgerman come to collect the debtor Parra family (hiding in the clinic as patients), Dar stands them off mid-stitch — the Blood Vein quoting from a bowl of water — and sends a counteroffer up the chain; Suri negotiates at the annexed Choratorium: formal sanctuary, both seals, no names leave the building, in exchange for uncounted free treatment of Gavel crews. Mother Gavel agrees too easily — and by morning her ledgermen are stationed across the lane with slates, counting the clinic's foot-traffic hour by hour; Suri opens a new ledger column: GAVEL — SANCTUARY (AGREED) / OBSERVED — DAILY, COUNTED — DAILY.

## Chapter 29 — The Ninth Petal
Index raiders hit the Saint Ferrous archive at the third bell to conscript/take hostage the surviving census clerks (Elara among the compliant); tithe-clerk Milla Ferun — who has heard the bounty naming "the Ferun printout" — defaults on her own roster for the first time in her life and escapes by the coal chute to the clinic's posted sanctuary. She surrenders the slip sewn into her coat lining: the only surviving print of Dar's RHYTHM UNRESOLVED readout, two rhythms on one slip, his own chest recorded before the Veins — and confesses she has drawn the five-petaled flower in her sleep for years; last night's flower had nine petals. Dar takes the printout into his coat with Vey's page; Milla is set to work counting freely at the front desk. Hook: salt again under her fingernails, though she has not been underground in weeks — she waits to see what the flower draws tonight.
