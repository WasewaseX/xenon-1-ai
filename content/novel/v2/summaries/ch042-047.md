# Summaries — Chapters 42–47 (Arc IV opening: the road and Lowmark)

## Chapter 42 — The Ledger of the Road
Dar, Suri (self-appointed porter, carrying Shirin's wax cylinders), and Vell of Brack walk the registry route in reverse across the salt flats beyond the seawalls — a landscape of cairns, grave-masts, and heat-berries, with Vell chalking the sea's accelerating retreat on the flower-marked route stones. The Lily dims under daylight (a tax) and is fed in the dark; her frost-petals point back toward Ashkarad and the deep heartbeat, and Dar's balance leans with her. At the drowned port's outskirts they find a single line of small, bare footprints crossing the exposed seabed inland toward Ashkarad, with no second set anywhere. Hook: Vell's chalk proves the prints are spaced exactly one heartbeat apart — the walker is keeping time to the buried Sister.

## Chapter 43 — The Drowned Choir
Lowmark is drowned to the second story; its seed has bloomed in the drained harbor as a district-sized black water-lily whose hum is a literal choir of the "married" (those the tide-priests fed to it, called marriages). Lodging with salvage-divers (Maren, Tetch), Dar treats divers free of charge while Suri counts the bloom-tallow economy: the seed's weeping is rendered, casked, and sold inland by caravan, and the marriages-in versus casks-out numbers expose a skim. They witness a marriage rite and the priests' commerce in comfort. Hook: after the wedding the priests sell "readings" from a relic kept in a lead box — a leaf of pressed memory-paper.

## Chapter 44 — Memory-Vellum
Dar trades a look at the relic from registrar Orlo, paying one small true memory (the smell of carbolic soap at Saint Ferrous) to the Registry's bone-and-brass press; the pressed payment slip arrives embossed with the flower. The relic proves to be a leaf of the World Registry itself — its pages are memory-velum, spent carrier-memories pressed and filed — and touching it gives Dar a dead bride's last morning, whole, ending with its own filing. At the moment of pressing he watches the Gardener's hand sign: left-handed, bone-white, a ring of old burn-scars at the wrist. Hook: she signs no name — the signature's flourishes are the flower sigil itself, her mark, over and over, like a stamp.

## Chapter 45 — True Currency
The registry's Lowmark house holds the deposit-copies of Ashkarad's page — Dar's five spent memories are on file and priced (the mother-entry written in the Gardener's black bone pen, priced at a decade). His first buyback: the press refuses his offered memory-bleed fragment as counterfeit — RHYTHM UNRESOLVED / ALREADY FILED — and accepts only true currency, so he spends the clinic's first morning to hear a playing of his father's laugh for eleven heartbeats (Suri counts his pulse and objects that the morning was partly hers). Inside the laugh a second voice is faintly humming the lullaby. Hook: his tone-deaf clerk father cannot have known that song — unless Dar's mother sang it where he could hear.

## Chapter 46 — Three Heartbeats of Her Face
The mother-entry is priced like a decade of a life; Vell offers herself as collateral under Brack's body-signing custom and Dar refuses, so Suri signs a promissory to the road itself — interest at the road's discretion, collected by the heralds. On account, the house shows three heartbeats of the face: everything is right until the eyes arrive — older than any mother's — and the Lily goes utterly still, verdict-like, because she holds the original pressed from Dar's memories at their fusion. As the face fades it mouths, soundlessly in a dead language he understands without hearing, an endearment he has never heard in his life: "little root." Hook: Suri quietly rules a new ledger column titled WHAT WE OWE THE ROAD.

## Chapter 47 — Bloom-Tallow
Lead: Suri. The tide-priests declare a marriage-tithe whose crop is Hessa, an eleven-year-old refugee stock-child sheltered by Maren's dive-house; Suri's granary audit (with Vell chalking a second copy) prices the priests' skim of the Bride's bloom and its sale to Gullet's traders. At the ceremony Dar's surgery breaks the rite open — the marriage-crown was already sutured into the child's scalp — and Suri reads the numbers from the pulpit stair, zeroing out the Bride's own share; the town of divers converts and the child is freed into their care, staying under Maren's roof as their ward (the claiming-hymn stirs in her blood and Dar breathes her down). That night the priests' granary burns itself, frost-petals first, green flame contained like an audit. Hook: the surviving traders' chit, stamped with the flower, bears the countersigned instruction — "per the Reclaim — sustain the route."
