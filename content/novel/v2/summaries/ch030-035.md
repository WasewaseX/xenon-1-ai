# Summary — Chapters 30–35

## Chapter 30 — One Beat Behind
Dar makes his first controlled Mind Vein use on the clinic roof, walking his swept memory-rooms without spending (mother's face, father's laugh, first kiss, home's color catalogued as emptied rooms) and declining again the furnished door he never built, which now shows moving lamplight beneath it. In the deepest hush he hears the second heartbeat clearly at last — patient, immense, and counting back exactly one beat behind his own, like a copyist reproducing his pulse one line down. The Cartographer's old tally shard still reads 109, but five fresh, unweathered strokes have been added beneath it that he does not remember cutting; the count only ever moves at thresholds. The Lily then leans toward the deep so hard his balance shifts bodily on the parapet — and something down there leans back, setting a counterweight against his sternum. Hook: the tally is not the ledger; it is the copy.

## Chapter 31 — The Salt-Glass Door
Against Vahid's written prohibition, Dar descends with Suri counting every step aloud and Ilay's copied spiral map in hand (Roya holds the shaft head, refusing to descend: "someone holds the top"). They go down by Milla's tithe-clerk route into the Marrowworks underbelly and the great Throne shaft — past donors' climbing chalk, the warning DO NOT COUNT THE HEARTBEATS, and the dead First Vessel's cold cathedral of flesh — to where the walls become translucent salt that rings like struck glass. At 711 steps the corridor ends at a door of salt-glass clear as deep water, behind which the second heartbeat fills the dark like a slow tide. Hook: pressed against the far side of the glass, level with Dar's chest, is a single black lace-veined petal.

## Chapter 32 — Petal Against Glass (ARC II CLIMAX)
The petal taps the salt-glass in the opening bars of the lullaby, and the Lily answers through Dar's ribs — the two songs reconcile line against line, frost blooming in matched five-petal patterns, the taps shifting to land on Dar's own offbeats — confirming the second Lily is real, alive, and aware of him specifically. Dar refuses to open the door (you do not corner the thing you came to meet; the opening must be her act, not a transaction she has not signed), the Lily yields, and the petal withdraws into the dark. The keyhole is left exposed — a five-petaled flower cut through the glass — and the door stands ajar, breathing warm, loam-scented air. Hook: the door was unlocked from the other side; she wants to be found and is afraid of being found — and above, on the attic desk, the Gardener's registry page waits.

## Chapter 33 — Eight Cities, One Route (ARC III opens)
In the clinic cellar, Dar lays Vey's registry page under Vahid's lens beside Roya's charts: eight names (Lowmark, the Vigil, Gullet, Sarn, Weep, Ossuar, Brack, Hallowfen), the flower sigil repeated like a surveyor's mark, Ashkarad circled — VESSEL: FLED—RECLAIM — and, in the paper's grain, a black pollen that is not ink. Roya finds the page has been needle-pricked like a tailor's pattern and the pollen came through the needle; Ilay says the page is warm "like the door." Plotting the pricked route resolves the eight names into an ordered collection round — Brack first (the Brack lane empty of League traffic), Ashkarad last — and Vahid names it: a creditor's itinerary for nine sown seeds, Dar carrying the collateral. Hook: lamp-warmed, every pollen grain turns slowly toward the sea; Roya calls the page "a receipt that knows where its debtor lives."

## Chapter 34 — The Surveyor's Kindness
With the storm-caravans comes Surveyor Coram Hesh of the Caravan League, who offers free structural surveys of storm-damaged districts and is everything Vey was not — humble, useful, apparently unpriced — and the clinic hosts him while the district takes him to its cook-fires. Suri catches him taking tissue-scrapings (wall fur and a curl of bloodied gauze) "for subsidence records," and confiscates one packet from the cellar. Dar hears Hesh humming at his work: the lullaby, note for note correct except for one small flat note sung where the true song leaves a hanging pause — forgeries fill blanks. Dar files the observation without reaction. Hook: that night every frost-bloom on the clinic's windows points, compass-true, at Hesh's lodgings by the tide-gates — the same bearing as the pollen.

## Chapter 35 — Harvest Marks
In the Gavel-annexed Choratorium, the Choirmaster resurfaces — alive, half-starved, guarding the reliquary corridors (and his re-labeled dead: Kaelen, Elara, Elias) from scavengers — when Roya arrives with rubbings of Hesh's survey marks. He goes grey reading them: harvest-routing marks, Church-grade inner-hand grammar (route, yield, gate, and claim marks) — the kind used to plan a city-wide tithe, and a master sheet exists behind them. He admits what he saved from the burning: one unburned copy of the harvest-registry, the vessel list entire, in a lead tithe-box — including "Kaveh, Suri, resold three times" — and keeps it: the reckoner will read it with him standing beside the shelf. Roya chalks the reconstructed master map on the archive floor, and the claim-mark — a circled five-petaled flower — lands on the clinic district. Hook: Ashkarad's page, being redrawn by hand; his old question "what is the city is for" reprised as a warning.
