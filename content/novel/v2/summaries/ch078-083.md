# Summaries — Chapters 78–83 (ARC VII "Throne of Salt": the testimony, the first carrier, the hymn, the readings, the charter, the bloom-fog)

## Chapter 78 — Her List of the Dead
Isra comes up the 711 steps after nine nights below with the Sister's gift: a sewn ledger of tallow-cured hide in the dead ledger-script — every city the Sister watched die, named and dated by the Root's heartbeat-counts, with the First-Burning world's dead, then the nine seeds' cities (eight BLOOMED, Brack stamped PRUNED, PAID, and Ashkarad's own column reading only LENT). The list is testimony prepared for a trial the Sister expects ("SOMEONE WILL. I WOULD RATHER IT WERE US."), and with it comes her request, delivered through Ilay's channel, entered by Suri in the census as the city's first unpriced debt: "If I go mad down there, sing the second verse, and I will remember I was loved before I was used" (singer: Ilay; standing order, no price attached). Closing hook: Milla finds the last entry is not a city but a boy — the carrier who fell — with an amendment added after a century, in ink nine nights old: "He did not die." Isra: the Sister added it eight nights ago, and then let her stop.

## Chapter 79 — The First Carrier
Two days later a white flag made of a single white glove goes up at the tide-gates, and Wen arrives — the fleet's Warden of the Reclaim, a century old, half-bloomed (black lace frozen mid-climb at his collar), signing his docket WEN, WARDEN OF THE RECLAIM, PER G. He knows Dar's accounts to the slip (account nine, closed at the counter's fee; "you funded our lamp-oil"), offers Dar's regrown original heart — beating in a clerk's case, slip pinned inside the lid: RHYTHM: STABLE. ASSET: ORIGINAL. HOLD — plus the schedule's mercy (the posted terms honored at the tail, after Hallowfen, a generation or two of grace) if Ashkarad kneels. Dar declines ("the tail is still a collection point"); Wen notes the refusal ("the books prefer refusals — they compound") and warns that the offer expires only when the tide remembers itself. He then shows the scar where the ninth seed slept before it fled him — a five-petaled keyhole socket below the lace — and confesses he is the first carrier, kept alive a century on the Gardener's books as her first failed key. Closing hook, cracking Dar worse than any threat: "Did it ever sing for you? It never once sang for me." Dar cannot answer; the Lily presses toward Wen and never makes a sound.

## Chapter 80 — The Hymn in Every Throat
The herald-chorus activates the claiming-hymn at first light from the seabed; the storm-walls conduct it inward (the locking-collar's own engineering), and every throat that ever learned the hymn wakes mid-bar — the eleven and a city's worth of sleeper-training, children standing up asleep to sing. Vahid fights it verse by verse: the chorus-free aerosol (Vigil-ash censers; Suri's breath-book already ruled, the seed of the ration), the needle-silence for the smallest, and the freed canal-singers under Lena Vance singing the hymn wrong on purpose up the waterways on the Choirmaster's re-tuned arrangement, while Elias leads the smaller children down the dead salt-tunnels out of range, lungs first, counting them through on breath. The final verse needs the full chord; the register is spent (nursery emptied, eleven underground, smallest out of range, streets smoked, canals singing back), and the hymn breaks the way debts stop. Closing hook: the heralds withdraw in step, unhurried, tally-bearers never stopping — the audit got what it came for: a complete census of who in Ashkarad can sing, with pitch, grade, and address.

## Chapter 81 — Read Aloud
Suri turns the enemy's census into the city's shield by attacking provenance: the fleet's appraisal rests on the Chorus's inherited ledgers, so for three days in Mother Gavel's quarry the clinic reads the books aloud — the rescued name-pages (day one, Suri reading her own retired page among them), Milla's original printout with the destruction-order read aloud and the forgeries ink-dated (a child's "Voluntary" consent dated years before the hand could write; one clerk's hand on four hundred consents in a week) — until the Index's own clerks defect with their stamps, beginning with Senior Clerk Ivet, who lays her forty-year Warden's stamp on the registrar's table ("The record should be kept by people who read it"); nineteen stamps by dusk, and the Index's docket dies of provenance. Day three the Choirmaster produces the unburned harvest-registry copy from the sealed clerk-nest under the harvest floor, reads every child's name into the open air with his own margin annotations, and burns it page by page: "No more vessels" (Sedge's backward count releases a unit; Gavel takes off her hat, unremarked). Closing hook: as the truth is counted the Root's rhythm beneath the city settles, slow and even — the deep, apparently, likes being read to (Isra's slate: THE DEEP HAS BEEN READ TO ALL ITS LIFE. NO ONE EVER READ IT THE TRUTH.).

## Chapter 82 — I Don't Get Kept
The fleet offers Mother Gavel the city's charter — Warden's Partner, the Reclaim's order-through-debt at scale, honest even in its appendices (DEFERRAL, NOT REMISSION) — and its beauty hides the trap on page nine: upon signature, every debt of her crews is assumed by the Reclaim, transferring her people into the Gardener's books forever. She reads it three times, carries it down to her own pit, and burns it before the assembled crews: "I keep ledgers. I don't get kept." The crews do not cheer; deputations come all night (pump-crew for, pit-crews against, a lamp-girl of seventeen asking if she stops owing when the book is taken — "no one signs my people over my signature"). Her price for joining the third way is the water-works, paid in function and never flesh (no new debts in the pipes; old ones retired her way under Suri's audit), and she hands Dar her brass speaking-tube as a loan "for the day the city must be counted in" — explicitly to be returned to her for the calling. Closing hook, her one condition flat as a ledger page: when the new order writes its books, her crews' debts go into the fire first — before the city's, before the Church's, before the deep's.

## Chapter 83 — The Bloom-Fog
The fleet stops negotiating: it tows the rendered carcass-barges of pruned Lowmark upwind of the city and burns bloom-tallow until the fog-sea that left with the ocean comes back from the landward side carrying hunger — every breath a craving for the deep, streets of people facing vault-ward, licking salt, counting downward in their sleep (Roya maps the fog's hands off Ilay's hearing; Isra's slate: THE SMOKE IS MY SISTER. THEY ARE BURNING MY SISTER; the deep, terribly, stays settled). Ashkarad seals — gates, flues, cistern air — and Suri rations the chorus-free censer-smoke by counted breaths: four breaths, twice a day, per soul, breath-books, no debts, no deferrals, the lines running on Gavel's water. On the fog's second night a fleet clerk stumbles against the seawall's outer face coughing in a four-beat cadence Suri has known since childhood — her brother Dathan, the counting-house boy who bought her skin back at nineteen with his own signature, now counting for the enemy; they speak once through the stone ("You still count, Su." / "Come to the gate." / "I've got a column to keep."), and she rules a line in her book and leaves the column empty — he is not filed. Closing hook: on the third night the tide comes back — uphill, wrong, no sea behind it — carrying the wain to the tide-gates like a coffin on a shoulder; the Lily hums one low note, like an acknowledgment of receipt.
