# Summary — Chapters 36–41 (Arc III finale: "The Gardener's Ledger")

## Chapter 36 — The Barge from Brack
A tithe-barge drifts against the ebb through Ashkarad's tide-gates crewed by dead Brack watermen, every surface furred with grey frost-petals that all turn to point inland at the city; Suri leads the boarding and finds the manifest is nothing — the barge is a delivered receipt. Among the dead her crews find one survivor: a tongueless senior counting-house clerk clutching a slate with an unfinished tally of Brack's dead, who survived by chalking herself into the death-column as zero. A Warden pulse-reader rigged to the wake has printed one census on an endless loop; Suri carries a slip to the clinic: BRACK POPULATION — 0. Closing hook: the receipt is addressed — Ashkarad is the next entry on the round, and the survivor left in the margin means someone still has an open column on her.

## Chapter 37 — What the Mute Carried
Dar risks the Mind Vein to read the survivor's memory-architecture, offering the "cheapest" room he owns, and pays the true fee: the ledger takes whole the memory of the first patient he ever lost — the name goes first, the burn-scarred hands fade; he keeps only the fact. Inside her stripped counting-house of a mind (shelves pressed to pale leaves), he sees Brack's harvest: white-gloved auditors who press memories and take counters' tongues as "redactions," the city's living Throne rooted in its harbor-temple with the seed cut from it like a tooth, and the final accounting signed left-handed with a five-petaled flower. Her last salvaged image, scored across her counting-house wall, closes the chapter: WE WERE COUNTED WORTH EATING. Afterward the clerk, Vell of Brack, chalks her first message to the living: she knows the water-marks and will count them there.

## Chapter 38 — Sibling Stock
Vahid cracks the Church's graft-lineage notation using the Choirmaster's unburned harvest-registry and Dar's copied vessel page, and confesses what it decodes: the nursery grew children from tissue drawn off the First Vessel for generations — the "sibling stock" — of which the eleven freed vessels and Ilay are the last cohort; Ilay is GRADE: PERFECT. When Ilay hums the deep music, the cellar's salt answers a half-step down, and Vahid realizes the deep is not messaging her but tuning to her — using Ilay as its tuning fork, striking the last cohort and counting what rings. Closing hook: Ilay says the music has "taught" her a new word — "it leaks, like under a door" — and writes it beneath the Nine Veins ladder: RECLAIM, and asks Vahid if it is a word for a debt. He says yes.

## Chapter 39 — Two Cups
At the Caravan League's feast in the Choratorium ruins (Mother Gavel's annexed hall), Hesh's month-long survey concludes and his master map — the clinic district marked with a circled five-petaled flower — hangs publicly over the head table, unreadable to almost everyone. Hesh serves Dar tea in two cups with Vey's exact ritual and her exact line — "You just have to decide which cup to drink from" — and Dar understands the ritual was handed down: the Church was a branch office of the Gardener's round. The Lily rages at Hesh's nearness so hard his gums split and the blood-crave spikes; Dar holds the door, drinks neither cup, and states the full understanding aloud to himself: the Lily is one of nine seeds, the collection round has begun, and Ashkarad is the anomaly being corrected. Closing hook: Hesh leaves his white gloves folded on the table between the untouched cups — patient, like something waiting to be worn again.

## Chapter 40 — The Listening Heart
Roya and the Choirmaster spring their trap: Dar's regrown original heart — recovered from Hesh's lodgings weeks before and rigged in the clinic cellar to a hand-cranked drum — has been Hesh's antenna all along, keyed to its flesh, and for nineteen days they fed it a rehearsed false rhythm (a "Schedule of Decline") into the salt. Stopping the drum lets the heart sing true, handing Hesh's masters a discrepancy only a deep verification can resolve; that night Hesh breaks cover in the graft-theater and calls the deep aloud — four bars of the lullaby with the one wrong note, then the stamped registry cadence. Every frost-bloom in the building turns on him like a jury; Dar watches from the altar-dark while holding the Lily still. Closing hook: exposed, Hesh does not run — he files his report to the open air, "Vessel located. Send the wain," and walks out past enforcers who cannot price the fee for touching him — and far below, for the first time since the salt-glass door, the deep heartbeat is no longer exactly one beat behind Dar's own.

## Chapter 41 — Beyond the Seawalls
The filing crosses the city by morning; with Hesh's room emptied (one coin left for Gavel's lamp-fee), the Index offering to sell the parish's irregulars, and the Truce of Salt ending at the seawalls, the council concludes Ashkarad cannot shelter a vessel that rings — so Dar chooses to walk the RECLAIM round in reverse and learn what the collection is for before the wain arrives. The farewells are priced and given: Ilay presses her sound-map of the deep into his hand for an unpriced promise, Suri appoints herself his porter ("You work for me now, Mehran"), Vahid loads him with Shirin's fourteen wax cylinders, the Choirmaster stays to guard the unburned harvest-registry, Roya and Milla see him to the stair, and Vell is already at the gate, having counted herself into the column. Closing hook: the seawalls open, Dar sees the fog-sea for the first time — and the tide is going out far too far, as if something at the horizon is drinking; the Lily leans once back toward the deep she is leaving, then forward, and Dar walks.
