# Summaries — Chapters 96–100 (ARC VIII finale: "The Ninth Vein")

## Chapter 96 — The Price of Rewriting
Eighth day: the named city convenes in the antechamber above the salt-glass door, the Gardener present with page zero in her glove. Before Dar can offer himself, the Sister volunteers through Isra's slate — the first seed, page zero, the only heart the books never held — and the Lily objects through Dar's body like a struck bell, sister against sister, until the ninth yields to the first. Dar's counter-offer is refused by the ledger itself (Suri reads him in six books: census, Standing Count rolls, common property, printout, clinic book, chord — "a chord cannot cosign"), and Wen, the century-kept first key, testifies to the difference between being kept and keeping. State change: the choice is made — the Sister will close the deep ledger from inside. Hook: her acceptance, the calmest writing Isra has ever done: "I HAVE BEEN ALONE BELOW SINCE THE BURNING. LET ME BE ALONE ON PURPOSE, FOR EVERYONE, ONCE."

## Chapter 97 — A Chord, Not a Rung
On the ninth day Vahid unchalks his own NO SONGS law and writes the true song's first bar in Shirin's notation; the Standing Count assembles above (Gavel's tube calling, Suri leading, Dathan beside her), and Dar and Isra climb Soul a final time, Isra's root carrying Ilay's transcription down to the Sister's pedal-tone. Vein Nine opens not as a rung climbed but as a chord struck — Dar does not bloom; every note remains itself in relation — and the deep ledger rewrites around consent: debts become shares, the stamps die into flowers, Sedge's backward count and the Cartographer's 109 tally close (no count can hold a chord). The Door beneath the salt opens once; the Gardener carries the dead First Vessel's remains through and lays her daughter in the grave that was always hers, then returns through, seven accounts yet to retire; Wen hears the ninth seed really sing at last. Hook: as the ledger closes, the Root — for the first time since the Burning — exhales.

## Chapter 98 — Some Things Were Meant to Be Shared
At the opened Door the reveals settle like accounts: the face Dar finally recovers is the Lily's mother's — the Gardener's — proving the "mother's lullaby" was memory-bleed all along, the Gardener's cradle song misfiled as inheritance (the Church's forty-year collar-song and Vey's tea-time humming were degradations of it; "little root" was never his mother's word). His true mother's face comes anyway — not from any ledger but from the city: Mrs. Gable's recorded stories and the neighbors' testimonies read into the chord by Suri and Milla, given, not bought, a new entry credited from others' remembering (the spent costs stay spent). Ilay hears the garden's true song whole, every verse and the uncounted ending, and the freed vessels under Elias sing it once through the opened Door for the grave. Hook: the Gardener, leaving the grave, says the line as benediction at last, not excuse — "Some things were meant to be shared."

## Chapter 99 — The One Who Stays
The great Door seals paid; the Sister withdraws below and takes the keeper's post — the chord's pedal-tone settled into the floor's grain, the rewritten ledger in her keeping — and the Gardener files page zero at last, folding her white gloves around it on the sill of the closing salt-glass door, then walks the dry seabed toward the seven far cities to sing the seven hungry Thrones down herself (resolution, not campaign). Wen's page closes with the voided Reclaim and he ends grateful at the tide-gate — a stopped clock finally allowed to stop — and the city burns its debts that night, Gavel's crews' first, per her standing bargain. At the sixth bell the city sings the Sister's requested second verse down the shaft, the pedal carries it once, and the second heartbeat goes still to every ear above the salt — pulse-readers printing ACCOUNT CLOSED. KEEPER: PRESENT. — not death, not corruption: permanent distance, priced. Hook: the Lily presses one black petal against the inside of Dar's chest, toward the deep, and holds it a long moment — a sister's hand on a sister's door.

## Chapter 100 — One Heartbeat, Many
Weeks later: the school of the unpriced runs full on Vahid's published honest text, Ilay is the city's cantor and sings without hearing machines, registry pages are re-stamped VESSEL: NO — CITIZEN: YES under Milla's eye, Roya carves the new city map on the Choratorium wall with her mask off, Milla dreams a green garden and wakes smiling with salt under her nails, and the Choirmaster finally hears the answer to his old question — the city is a lid, a lock, and, latterly, a home. On the roof in the first true salt storm since the Blacktide, Dar counts one heartbeat that is somehow many — his, the Lily's fraction that stayed, the counted, the makers', the deep's slow consent below — and does not spend a thing to hear it; across the alley the old five-petal census mark has been redrawn with nine connected petals: a census, a count, and no one missing. Closing hook — the single conscious thread left open: far away, in seven far cities, seven seeds turn over in their sleep and begin, very slowly, to wake.
