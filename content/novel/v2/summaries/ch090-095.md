# Summaries — Chapters 90–95 (ARC VII climax into ARC VIII, the three-way choice)

## Chapter 90 — Both, or Neither
Dawn after the Standing Count: HUNGRY still in the walls, the Cow's scream burned to a whine, the herald-chorus conductorless. The Root bargains up every crack in the daughter's mimicked voice ("Feed me... open me... we are both owed" — the voice Dar has spent a year calling his mother's, skipping every ninth beat, never singing the song's ending), while the Gardener comes in person at last — walking the dry seabed through the bloom-fog, bone-white hands bare, burn-scarred wrists, received at the gate-reader by the deep's own rhythm — to ask rather than command: open the Door with her, and let her bury her daughter in the garden she was stolen from. At the vault-mouth convergence (Suri, Gavel, Choirmaster, Wen, Isra, Roya, Ilay, Dar), the Sister's answer comes up through Ilay like a verdict: her daughter is not in the grave — her daughter is the seed in Dar's chest. ARC VII CLIMAX closes on the Lily's first spoken words since the epilogue, aloud, through Dar's mouth and her own: "Both. Or neither."

## Chapter 91 — The True Books
Suri takes delivery of Wen's defection: the Warden of the Reclaim arrives at the tide-gates with a hand-cart of the fleet's round-account ledgers ("It sang. That was my fee... The books are the balance, and I am done"), and sits down beside his folded white flag to await the city's judgment. Auditing the beautiful, calm books (Vell chalking, Milla certifying), Suri finds Brack closed PRUNED, PAID, Lowmark's account still open by a carcass, and seven bloomed cities still on the true schedule (the Vigil, Gullet, Sarn, Weep, Ossuar, Hallowfen, Lowmark to be finished). At dusk Dathan Kaveh defects with the second set — the true schedule he countersigned for eleven years until his sister's census shamed him — including the Reclaim's final provision for Ashkarad: open the Door from within within nine days, "On failure: prune, and uncount" (no yield, no line-item, no name kept). Hook: the true books' last page is memory-velum in the Gardener's own left hand, flower sigil for a signature, and beside the Root's account, in fresh ink, a private line: "it no longer knows my song."

## Chapter 92 — What the Daughter Was
The revelation assembles from the Lily's own pulses (nine pulses, rest, nine — "She is not keeping time. She is keeping a family"; Roya's chalked genealogy of nine circles, one shaded) and the Church's sibling-stock ledgers: every seed in the World Registry and every child in the Church's registry drawn on the same account — the daughter's. At the salt-glass door the Sister deposes once, through Isra (warmth paid per letter) and written in slate: the Gardener's daughter died at the First Burning as the Root woke; locked above it as a living lid; and the Gardener went into the dying and cut nine seeds from her — "I AM THE FIRST. SHE IS THE LAST. WE ARE ALL OF US THE DAUGHTER." The Gardener's apology arrives through every bloom in the vault at once, like a sentence rehearsed for a century: "Some things were meant to be shared. I shared her the only way I had left." Hook: Ilay, doing the honest child's arithmetic, says quietly that this makes the eleven children her cousins — and no one in the room can find a reason to correct her (the Sister's follow-up through the music: she has been counting them all since the Burning, and wants to know if the cousins can visit).

## Chapter 93 — The Honest Text
Vahid, one-handed in the clinic cellar with the fourteen cylinders and nine days on the city's clock, stops listening to Shirin's recorded words and finally reads her hums: pitch-true, staved, ordered — the honest text of the upper Veins, which Shirin heard a test-seed sing once in their workshop decades ago and hid in the rest-gaps of her own voice where no auditor looks. The notation matches Ilay's transcription note for note — two witnesses a century apart — and its doctrine is not a ladder: the eighth and ninth Veins layer, and the Throne is a harmony of carriers, Flesh through Throne struck together ("the audition is the score"; Ilay's nine-petal chalk flower was the theorem first). He completes the chorus-free protocol, rules the line closed — THE LINE CLOSES HERE. THE LILY IS THE LAST SEED OF THE STRAIN. — and, changing to a lighter needle at half weight, plays the hairline second groove inside the fourteenth cylinder's voice-band: her younger voice finishing the sentence cut a year ago — "and then you will hear the sound—" / "—is the share."

## Chapter 94 — Order, or Hunger
The city's last factional storm convenes in Mother Gavel's quarry (Choirmaster POV; the pit's honest acoustics): the Index under Seft and Gavel's remnant (Harl) press option one — a new Gardener, a kinder ledger, the pruning of the seven remaining bloomed cities continued with mercy; the Hammers' remnant (Dree) and the fog-sick press option two — burn the garden, free every seed worldwide into hunger, let the world take its chances. The count deadlocks even twice until the Choirmaster breaks the tie at his own freight: order is "a filing system for hunger" (he graded the harvest, tuned the compliance-hymns, signed the receipts), hunger eats kindly or eats everything, and he will vote for the thing he has never once seen administered — consent; "I give the roll back to the singers." Hook: the vote carries for the third way at dawn, fog-sick benches included — and beneath the quarry the deep heartbeat slows, like something listening to its own fate being decided.

## Chapter 95 — The Warden's Chair
Dar walks into the fleet camp alone, carrying nothing (everything sealed at the clinic — "Bring back a receipt"), past the conductorless chorus and the whining Cow, and is received into the wain's counting-room, where the Gardener offers him the seat formally: the working chair facing the world's ledgers — the Root fed on schedule by his hand, the seven cities stewarded kindly, the world ordered and living — the chair Vey died reaching for, "sized at last... for a man who pays his debts." He declines and names the third way (the ledger rewritten around consent; debts into shares; the Door opened on a chord, not a hand), and she prices the rewrite like an auditor: "a ledger closes only on a life in it: someone stays below, in the book, forever" — no interest, no remission, no transfer, no visiting column. Then she unfolds from inside her glove page zero — the first leaf of the World Registry, memory-velum, blank, never filled, never filed, cut for her eldest — and Dar walks back down the seabed already knowing, from the Sister's own tally (everything counted since the Burning except herself), which heart in Ashkarad the books have never once held.
