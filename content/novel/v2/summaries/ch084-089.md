# Summaries — Chapters 84–89 (ARC VII "Throne of Salt": the heart, the testimony, the crack, the Standing Count)

## Chapter 84 — Collateral Returned
With the tide gone and the deadline spent, Wen walks in under the white flag a second time and returns Dar's original heart in its glass as "collateral restored" — a courtesy with a mechanism inside: Vahid finds the heart re-keyed to the deep's rhythm (one heart, three chambers, counting back in threes) and primed to sing the water-borne claiming-song through the city's cisterns and pump-works the next time the ninth seed feeds. Dar, alone with the case in the dark, has to bleed his palm to turn the Lily's craving aside (blood craved when Dar bleeds; she wanted his original heart). Hook: the heart begins to sing the lullaby in a forged composite of his mother's voice — assembled from the music-memories the Gardener bought — and Dar, holding his own heart, files the finding: it begs, and it was never her.

## Chapter 85 — Burial as Gift
The war-room splits (Vahid: fire; Gavel: lead cage; Suri: the ch40 false-rhythm trick) until the Sister's counsel comes up through Ilay: "LET HER EAT IT. THE FIRST SEED REMEMBERS EVERY HEART. SHE WILL KEEP YOURS SAFE BELOW." Dar rules it under sanctuary terms and descends with Vahid (carrying the case), Suri, and Isra; at the wide-open salt-glass door he gives his original heart to the first seed, which takes it, unwinds the primed verse, and buries it in the vaults beside the daughter's cold chamber — grave-goods and a pass-key, the bomb defused by grief. The Lily's hum settles into the Sister's key — sisters dividing an estate. Hook: up the shaft comes the Sister's first unprompted words, transcribed by Ilay: "NOW I OWE YOU. I HATE OWING." (Ilay's postscript: owing is a debt; she pays her debts; it just takes her a hundred years to open the account.)

## Chapter 86 — Testimony at the Door
Dar brings the council down — Suri, Gavel, the Choirmaster, Milla as witnesses (Roya/Vell holding the shaft head, Ilay as the channel, Isra as the Vessel) — and the Sister testifies at the salt-glass door: the century, the locking ("no malice, only arithmetic"), the sowing, the prunings she counted, and the entry the city needs: NOT EVIL — LONELY; and afraid of the Gardener, because the Gardener is afraid too — the Root stopped answering her decades ago and she has been feeding it blind on her heralds' books ever since, while listening down the collar for a voice that stopped being hers. Hook, delivered twice (once through Ilay, once by the deep itself, without the girl, for the first time): "She thinks she still hears my mother. She hears the Root imitating her."

## Chapter 87 — The Crack in the Ninth
To see the Door before the fleet forces it, Dar and Isra climb Soul again at the rig (tithe paid: the clinic awning's color; a small fish's name), and Dar pushes past the shared door — Vein Nine, Throne, opens a crack: not bloom-through, because he is not one; the census of the willing is held in him the way a chord holds a note. Through the crack he sees the Door beneath the salt, older than the Church and the burning, and beyond it the first seed's original garden — green, tended, alive — where a white-gloved woman (burn-scarred wrists) kneels at a grave that is empty. Hook: the keeper looks up straight through the crack and mouths the endearment from the three-heartbeat face: "little root." Aftermath: the crack does not close (a wound that sees does not close), the pulse-reader prints NINE (9) RHYTHMS RESOLVED, an hour of the climb is spent, and the Lily goes still, then leans toward the deep and offers her four notes.

## Chapter 88 — What the Harvest Was For
At a third parley Wen confirms the geometry: the fleet does not want Ashkarad punished — it wants the Door opened, and only a living Throne can open it from inside (the Choirmaster reads the Ordinal's oldest clause on-page), the harvest having always been sedation to keep the Root dreaming while the key is grown; the posted terms were a planting bed, and Vey was the Gardener's subcontractor, filed as a defaulted line. Wen's century-old confession, verbatim: "She doesn't want a Throne. She wants her daughter's door unlocked. I was the first key she tried. I jammed." Hook: for the fleet's true books — where every seed is entered by its song and the ninth's entry reads Song: unverified — Wen asks, before he ends, whether the ninth seed can really sing.

## Chapter 89 — The Standing Count
The fleet forces the vault-mouth with the wain's engine-seed (Gullet's Cow, weaponized, moaning like something begging) and the herald-chorus in ranks under the bone-masked prologue captain — the hymns' conductor, a failed carrier who never stopped performing the liturgy — and Ashkarad answers with the Standing Count: every soul above the vault giving heartbeats freely in rhythm, Gavel's brass tube calling the count, Suri leading, the chorus-free children singing Ilay's transcription ("for my grandmother") in the antechamber, the freed voices under the streets, the Sister's pedal-tone below, and Dar at the center holding Vein Nine's crack open like a chest he refuses to let bloom shut. The claiming-hymn cannot activate what is already singing together, and the conductor is drowned in his own redirected choir — found silent, kneeling, gloves empty; Gavel rules the line paid and the mask a headstone. Hook: the Cow's engine screams against the chord, and the Root wakes enough to say one word up every crack in the city (printed by every pulse-reader, chalked a foot high by Ilay): HUNGRY.
