# Summaries — Chapters 60–65 (end of ARC V "Sister" into ARC VI "The Soul Door")

## Chapter 60 — Flag of Truce, Black Petal
On the third night of the watch at the shaft mouth, the Sister — still standing at the threshold of the wide-open salt-glass door without crossing — sends a single black petal up the 711 steps of the shaft like a flag of truce; via Isra's slate she petitions Ashkarad for ASYLUM, FROM MY MOTHER — "I DO NOT INVADE. I DEFECT." — and Dar pockets the petal with Vey's registry page and Milla's printout. Before the city can answer, a six-strong herald team enters by the old canal-works past the dry tide-gates, cannot be tolled or touched, and shows the Gavel customs post the wain's chained tally-book — a round-account that precedes the wain itself. Lena Vance's freed voice screams the warning down every waterway at once ("GLOVES IN THE CANALS. THEY CARRY THE BOOK."). Hook: the tally-book's first stamped line is BRACK — PRUNED, PAID.

## Chapter 61 — The Claiming-Hymn
Vahid, working nine weeks from Shirin's fourteen wax cylinders, has distilled six numbered vials of the countermeasure — a "rest" harvested from the silences in Shirin's own recordings; chorus-free medicine, a counter-singing that sings to nothing. The council evacuates the eleven stock children and Ilay from the marked clinic district through the Choirmaster's dead reliquary corridors, and the heralds strike both ends at once — a team marking the Throne shaft's mouth, three entering the corridor and singing the activation verse of the claiming-hymn. Elias (lungs graded PERFECT) answers against his will, the whole cohort beginning to wake to the tune, until Vahid injects the first dose mid-verse and the hymn dies "the way debts stop"; the heralds, probe complete, withdraw untouched. Hook: they leave the tally-book behind, open to Ashkarad's page: LIEN PENDING — COLLATERAL: THE LID (the book goes to the clinic/Choirmaster's keeping).

## Chapter 62 — The Nine Locks (ARC V CLIMAX)
The deep stirs deliberately at the sixth bell and every pulse-reader in Ashkarad prints the same vast rhythm, breaking the Warden tracking layer and Ilay's two circles merges into one ("together now"). At the antechamber of the open door, the Sister's testimony via Isra's slate and Ilay's hearing completes the geometry: the city is a lid; the vaults hold nine lock-seals she has counted by touch for a century; the nine sown seeds are their keys; eight keys are already turning in the far cities (Lowmark, the Vigil, Gullet, Sarn, Weep, Ossuar, Hallowfen, Brack), and the ninth key sleeps in Dar's chest — with her note that the Root's heartbeat skipped one a week ago ("dying or waking; she did not know which entry to post it under"). The Gardener's standing offer is relayed — the Eighth Vein wants two climbers, a door shared between two, and the Gardener will climb it with whoever says yes — and Dar chooses Isra, the Sister's Vessel; she writes I CLIMB; Suri appoints herself counter of the door. Hook: Isra's slate for Dar alone: "She says: bring the ninth's music. Bring nothing you cannot lose."

## Chapter 63 — Shirin's Marginalia
Vahid concedes his papers end at Bone (Mind opened as a wound, not a text) and finally plays Shirin's cylinders through; her voice-dropped "marginalia" yield the theory — Soul is a shared organ, grown between two people like a bridge between banks, grafted along the line where two silences fit; "the door is shared between two or it is not a door." The clinic builds the climbing-rig over the salt-glass door: a canvas-and-brass cold room a body's width off the glass, two chairs, the old stolen pulse-reader (the first machine ever to print RHYTHM UNRESOLVED at Dar), and the chalked law NO SONGS / NOT EVEN HUMMING; Shirin's ninth-cylinder rule is posted — what is pooled is pooled forever. Hook: the fourteenth (last) cylinder ends on a clean cut, mid-word: "and then you will hear the sound—" — the identical truncation as the Lily's oldest dream ("the same knife"; "the cut is upstream of both of them").

## Chapter 64 — The Shared Room
First session: Dar and Isra enter Mind-first, find the seam between their architectures, and build a room in the between-space, paying in deliberately — Dar trades the Blacktide refusal (the offered salt garden and his No.; held, not spent), Isra trades the drowned flats of her childhood — and the founding rule is learned: memory given here is not spent but held in common, unownable by either (Suri's slip: ROOM HOLDS. FLOOR: HERS. WALLS: HIS. OWNERSHIP: NONE.). The old machine prints RHYTHM UNRESOLVED, its fault finally a reading. Second night, Isra goes down ahead and finds the lamp lit and a third heartbeat under the floor — the Sister, listening like an ear pressed to a wall from below; she does not speak. Hook: she rearranges the room's furniture — chairs to a bench, table to a planting-frame, angles of tended ground — to how it stood in a garden neither climber remembers, and Isra answers with a diver's sign: her slate placed, unmarked, where the rearrangement made a place for it.

## Chapter 65 — The Gavel Ration
With the sea still gone, Mother Gavel seizes the pump-works and institutes the Gavel ration — WATER FOR WORK, chits and bucket-lines whose arithmetic Suri audits and finds cruel and true; the Index (Seft) files a writ demanding the stock children as conscripted sonar (the clinic refuses: "They're patients" — stamped RECEIVED, NOT COMPLIED), and the Open Counts splinter three ways over whose census is true. The clinic declares neutral ground and posts the terms: Suri runs the sanctuary census with Milla Ferun as registrar and Vell chalking the second copy, both factions' seals on it — and Dar disperses the clinic's sensitive archive among its witnesses, returning Milla's printout to her coat lining ("Evidence keeps best with its witness"). Hook: auditing the clinic's exemption, Gavel's ledgermen open a Chorus vital-debt master volume bought for pennies from the burned census-hall salvage and find KAVEH, SURI — resold three times, final assignment of record: the Gavel. Mother Gavel at the door: "Sanctuary keeps names in. It's never once kept a debt out. I'll send terms." Suri rules a new column: THE GAVEL — OWNS — ME?
