# Summaries — Chapters 48–53 (Arc IV finale: the Vigil and Gullet; Arc V opens)

## Chapter 48 — The Walking Quiet
Five days out of Lowmark the party's frost turns to point ahead, and the Vigil comes over the horizon: a walled monastery-city walking its decades-long circuit on "pilgrim-legs" built from coffin-cells of the Order's filed dead, silent by rule (nothing above a whisper). The Silent Order's monks read the party's frost, and the Abbot (unnamed by vow) hears Dar's two hearts and names him "one of the nine," offering hospitality on one condition — lead-glass quarters, "for the world's safety," indefinitely; Dar counters with nights in the glass plus medicine. State changes: Vell is fluent for the first time since Brack (chalk is the city's language); the Lily strains toward the Vigil's seed and is held. Hook: the guest-rite ends at the founder's tomb — a lead cradle built to hold the registry's first page, page zero — and it is empty; a herald took it within the decade ("the road took its own first page back").

## Chapter 49 — Terms of Silence
Inside the lead-glass cell Dar works out the quarantine as a hostage exchange: the Vigil's seed is not asleep but listening through their silence, so the Order dare neither sing nor stop singing (a two-century whisper; the seed woke once when a novice sang at his grandmother's filing). Dar treats the whisperers' ruined throats and lungs, and the Abbot pays in truth: the seed listening; the Gardener's route (the Order's tally-wall log — the round confirmed, Brack first, the Vigil passed over because "the road does not eat its glass-house," its tithe being lead panes); the wain's schedule (logged at Sarn eleven days ago, at Gullet within the month, Ashkarad the last name); and the word for Dar's condition — "not vessel: scion," a cutting the seed chose. The Order also reports the deep heartbeat passed beneath the wastes a month ago toward Ashkarad (they call her the Pilgrim), and that their seed has strained after it ever since — the quarantine is failing. Hook: the last scion, three circuits past, walked into the fog-sea off the circuit's jetty and left white gloves folded on the end post, still there, untouchable.

## Chapter 50 — The Renderworks
Gullet is a reef-city grown on a beached leviathan's carcass — ribs for gantries, baleen for walls — where bloom-tallow (including Lowmark's skim) is rendered in open works into light, glue, and tallow. Suri (lead POV) audits the company in an afternoon with Vell chalking a second copy on the factor's wall: the "book of the light" shows bloom-debt signed at fourteen, scrip spendable only at the company store, interest every salt-moon, and — the finding — no entry stamped PAID in sixty years: four generations mortgaged to rendering themselves, while the company pays the route in flower-stamped chits ("sustain the route"). Dar runs a free ward (bloom-scald frosts its victims) and treats factor Doane's foot to buy the access. Hook: at shift-change the city's heart — a brass-and-glass womb the foremen call the Cow — moans "like something begging," and Suri logs: THE ENGINE BEGS AT SHIFT-CHANGE. ENGINES DO NOT BEG. SOMETHING DOWN THERE IS OWED.

## Chapter 51 — Old Tally
A day-chain parts under load in Gullet's yard (eleven hurt, one render-hand dead of bloom-scald; the company bills the corpse's lost shifts), and foreman Coss conscripts Dar — the only surgeon on the reef — into the engine-room because the chain-tail has fouled the Cow's extraction-line, which must be treated, not cut. In the carcass's chest Dar finds the Cow's true source: Old Tally of Gullet, the third seed's first and only carrier, half bloom-through (black lace to the collarbone, a garden-arm seated in the extraction-glass), chained as the living extraction-point for a hundred years, counting every cask. She reads the Lily through his coat in one glance and calls it by the family pet-name "little ninth." Hook: at shift-change the moan is a call "in one direction only" — Tally says the seed cries for her sister, and that she has waited a hundred years to ask a ninth one question, and the question is about her sister.

## Chapter 52 — A Door Shared Between Two (ARC IV CLIMAX)
Over a week of night visits, Tally's question is asked — "Is there anything of my sister behind it?" — and her story delivered: seventy years ago a sister-carrier walked out of the wastes to the Cow's song; Tally hid her six weeks (family, no honest texts); the woman tried the Eighth Vein alone under the carcass and died against a door "that isn't shut." Tally gives the doctrine the honest texts lack: the Soul Vein is not a rung — it is a door shared between two, opened from the far side by someone who knows the shape of your silence, never by one; the door checks your company, not your fortune. Dar understands the deep heartbeat has spent a year learning exactly that — one beat behind, tapping his offbeats — so the far-side opener is the Sister. Tally frees a black petal of the third seed at cost and presses it into his hand "to show your Sister" (message: the third kept the light burning; the Cow is sorry), warns the wain passed Gullet a week ago bound for Ashkarad, and refuses rescue (unchaining kills her, the seed, and the city's light; Dar files the debt: "I owe you a door's asking"). Hook: at shift-change the deep heartbeat rolls in under the reef from the south and past — the Sister ahead of them, running home — and the Cow's moan breaks pitch to answer; frost points back along the road.

## Chapter 53 — Go Home, Ninth (ARC V opens)
The party turns homeward (wain ahead; doctrine pointing home; Suri's road-debt "done my owing where the collateral lives"), and the flats are wrong: the fog-sea is gone entirely from the seawalls — a dry horizon, no tide-smell, heat-berry stands banked to embers. On the third day home the mud is scored for a mile with ledger-script in the dead language Dar understands without hearing; matched against Ilay's sound-map it renders in one line — GO HOME, NINTH — an addressee-line (address, or stamp), scored by the barefoot walker whose prints shorten along it and turn toward the walls. At the seawalls the wall's base is breached from below: a sixty-foot section of foundation lifted whole and set aside, "not siege, passage" — the Sister went under the city through her own deep door while they were away, the passage rimed with petals pointing down and in; Ashkarad beyond is armed, smoking, and mid-arithmetic. Hook: from the breach Dar looks back and the scored heading's last stroke glistens wet along one edge, as if the writer stood there within the hour — she is ahead of them, and the ninth is expected.
