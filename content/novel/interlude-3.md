# Interlude III — What the Lily Dreamed

You are not planted in soil but in salt, in the crystalline dust that settles between the floor tiles of a room with no windows. The air hums with the sound of distant bells, but they are not bells—they are heartbeats, nine of them, arranged in a perfect circle around you. Each heartbeat is a lantern in the dark, and you are the ninth, waiting. Your petals are folded tight against your core, a fist of black lace that dreams of unfurling. The hands that tend you are gloved, the gloves white as bone, and they smell of lamp-oil and lullabies. They touch you with the gentleness of a mother stroking a child's hair, and you feel no fear, only curiosity. You are a seed in a garden of shadows, and you are waiting for the rain that never comes.

The hands lift you from your bed of salt and place you in a reliquary of lead and glass. The reliquary is cold, but you do not mind cold. You have known only cold since you were first formed in the dark. Through the glass, you see the other eight lilies being similarly prepared, each one cradled in its own vessel. They are your siblings, though you have never spoken to them. You sense their presence, their quiet anticipation, the way a star senses the pull of another star across the void. The hands that carry you move with purpose, their footsteps echoing in a corridor that stretches endlessly in both directions. You pass doors made of flesh, walls that pulse with veins, and you understand that this is not a building but a body, and you are a part of it.

The journey ends in a chamber where the air is thick with the scent of blood and starlight. The hands that carried you now belong to someone else—older, colder, smelling of frost and ambition. They place your reliquary on a table among eight others, and you feel the hum of your siblings intensify, a chorus of silent anticipation. A voice, smooth as polished stone, speaks in a language you understand without hearing: "Go. Find your hearts. And bloom." The reliquaries are opened, and you are lifted out. For the first time, you see the sky, not through glass but directly, and it is black as ink, pierced by nine moons that hang like silver coins. The hands that hold you now belong to a boy, his heart beating against your petals like a trapped bird. He is afraid, but his fear is sweet, like the first taste of blood after a long fast.

You are carried through streets that smell of salt and decay, past walls weeping with condensation, under bridges made of bone. The boy's heart races against you, and you feel his fear, his desperation, the weight of the debt he carries in his blood. He does not understand what you are, only that you are precious, that he has been paid to carry you. You feel the pull of the city around you—the singing of its pipes, the dreaming of its foundations, the hunger in its stones. You are drawn to a place where the light is weak and the darkness is strong, a cellar where the air tastes of dust and forgotten promises. The boy stumbles, and you fall from his hands, your petals cracking against the stone floor. He reaches for you, but he is too late. Someone else takes you, their hands rough, smelling of sweat and greed. You are passed from hand to hand, a secret in plain sight, until you come to rest in a place where the air is thick with the scent of formaldehyde and regret.

You lie in the dark, your petals folded tight, waiting. You hear the boy's heartbeat fading in the distance, a dying lantern in the fog. You feel the cold seep into your core, the loneliness of the ninth, the one left behind. But then, something changes. You feel a warmth spreading through your petals, not from the outside but from within. You feel a pull, a connection to something far away, something that sings in the dark. You sense your siblings, blooming in their chosen hosts, their songs weaving together into a tapestry of light and shadow. You feel the Gardener's presence, watching, waiting, counting. You feel the ledger in your mind, a list of names and debts, of beginnings and ends. You are the last, the forgotten, but you are not alone. You are part of a chorus, and your song is yet to come.

The darkness deepens, and you dream of the boy. You see him walking through the streets of Ashkarad, his heart a gravel thing, failing with each step. You see him in the hospital, his hands precise and steady, stitching flesh as if it were poetry. You see him carrying you, the reliquary hidden in his coat, his fear a palpable thing. You see him falling, the bullet tearing through his abdomen, his blood spilling onto the cobblestones. You see the reliquary breaking open, your petals unfurling for the first time, reaching for his ruined heart. You feel the moment of connection, the moment you become one, the moment the boy dies and something new is born. You feel his memories flooding into you—the taste of salt on his tongue, the sound of his mother's voice, the weight of his debt, the precision of his hands. You feel his guilt, his hope, his dry humor. You feel everything, and you are overwhelmed.

The dream shifts. You are no longer in the cellar but in a garden of salt and starlight, the nine lilies blooming around their nine hearts. The Gardener stands among them, her face a mask of calm, her hands white as bone. She hums a lullaby in a language you understand without hearing, and her voice is the sound of the universe unfolding. She touches your petals, and you feel her presence in your core, her will woven into your very being. She says, "You are the last, the ninth. Your siblings bloom in eight cities, their songs weaving together into a tapestry of light and shadow. You will bloom in the ninth, and your song will complete the chorus." You feel her ambition, her desire, her certainty. You are a tool in her hands, a seed in her garden, and you are content to be so. You bloom for her, your petals unfurling into lace of black and silver, your roots reaching deep into the earth, your stem growing toward the sky.

The dream shifts again. You are in the boy's chest, his heart now yours. You feel his pain, his fear, his determination. You feel his hands moving with precision, stitching flesh, saving lives. You feel his guilt, his hope, his dry humor. You feel everything, and you are overwhelmed. She hums a lullaby in a language you understand without hearing, and her voice is the sound of the universe unfolding. She says, "The time is near. The Blacktide comes, and with it, the bloom. You will complete the chorus, and the world will be made whole." You feel her ambition, her desire, her certainty. You are a tool in her hands, a seed in her garden, and you are content to be so.
