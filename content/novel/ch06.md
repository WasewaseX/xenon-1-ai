# Chapter 6 — The Cartographer

The Undergleam smelled of lamp oil and damp paper, a place where light was rationed like every other resource in Ashkarad. Dar followed Suri through tunnels so narrow their shoulders brushed the slick, salt-crusted walls, past stalls where shadow merchants traded in rumors and contraband organs preserved in jars of cloudy fluid. Suri's carbon-scaled arm scraped against a pipe, making a sound like stone on stone. "Three turns left," she said, counting under her breath. "Then look for the stall that smells of ink and old blood. The Cartographer doesn't like being late, and she hates being kept waiting more."

The air grew colder as they descended deeper, the salt-storm fog above pressing down like a physical weight. Dar felt the Lily in his chest stir, a subtle pulse that matched the rhythm of his own heartbeat but with an alien cadence. It had been quiet since they'd left Professor Anousheh's, but now it seemed to wake, curious about the direction they were taking. "What exactly are we paying with?" Dar asked, keeping his voice low. The Lily didn't like loud noises; they made it withdraw, leaving him feeling hollow.

"Information," Suri said. "And trust. The Cartographer deals in both. She'll want to know what the Lily dreams." She paused at a junction where three tunnels converged, each marked with different symbols scratched into the wall—a spiral, a cross, a stylized heart. "Left," she said decisively. "The other two are watched."

The stall was nestled in a alcove where the natural seepage of the Undergleam had formed a shallow pool of brackish water. A woman in a brass mask that covered her face from forehead to chin sat behind a table covered in parchment. Her gloved hands moved with practiced precision, drawing lines that shimmered and rearranged themselves as she worked. The air around her hummed with a faint energy, like static electricity after a storm.

"The Cartographer," Suri said by way of introduction. "We have what you want."

The woman looked up, her eyes visible through the slits in the mask—dark and sharp, like obsidian chips. "And you have what I need," she said, her voice a low contralto that seemed to echo slightly. "The dreamer. Bring him closer."

Dar approached cautiously, aware that he was being assessed. The Lily in his chest was restless now, a fluttering against his ribs that made him short of breath. "What exactly will you be doing?" he asked.

The Cartographer gestured to a chair of woven reeds that looked uncomfortable but sturdy. "I will be listening to what your guest has to say. Through you." She held up a small device made of brass and wire, intricate and somehow alive. "It's called a song-lobe. I'll wire it to your temples. You'll sleep, and I'll map what comes through."

Dar hesitated. Sleep meant vulnerability, and vulnerability in a city like Ashkarad was a debt waiting to be called in. "What's the price?" he asked.

The Cartographer smiled, a thin line visible behind her mask. "Not money. Not yet. Information. What you know about the Church, about the Lily, about the man who sent you to Professor Anousheh. And what the Lily dreams—that's what I really want."

Dar exchanged a glance with Suri, who gave a slight nod. "It's the fastest way to what we need," she said. "The Cartographer's maps are worth more than any currency in the Undergleam."

Dar sat in the reed chair, wincing as the metal contacts of the song-lobe touched his temples. The Cartographer's gloved fingers were surprisingly gentle as she adjusted the wires. "Close your eyes," she instructed. "Let go. The Lily will speak through you."

The darkness behind Dar's eyelids wasn't empty; it was filled with the Lily's presence, a pulsing, living darkness that reached out to touch his mind. He felt himself sinking, falling through layers of consciousness, the Lily's dreams rising to meet him.

He was standing in a garden made of salt, under a sky the color of dried blood. The ground was crystalline, and beneath his feet, he could feel the slow, rhythmic pulse of something vast and sleeping. Rows of people lay buried in the earth like bulbs, their heads protruding, eyes closed in peaceful slumber. A tower rose in the distance, pulsing with light—each window a heartbeat, each floor a different rhythm. And from the tower, a voice hummed, a lullaby in a language Dar didn't know but somehow understood.

The Cartographer's voice, distant and muffled, intruded on the dream. "Draw it," she said to someone—perhaps herself, perhaps an unseen assistant. "The salt gardens, the sleeping rows, the tower of hearts. And the voice—can you map the frequency?"

Dar tried to speak, to ask where he was, who was humming, but the dream held him fast. The Lily was in complete control here, showing him what it wanted him to see. A figure approached through the garden—not walking, but gliding, her robes trailing behind her like smoke. Her face was obscured, but her presence was familiar, like a half-remembered melody. She stopped before one of the sleeping figures and placed a hand on their forehead, humming a single, perfect note that made the salt crystals tremble.

"Who is she?" Dar managed to whisper, though his voice was lost in the dream.

The figure turned, and for a moment, Dar thought he recognized the eyes—Anra Vey's eyes, calm and ancient. But then the dream shifted, and the garden dissolved into darkness.

Dar woke with a start, gasping for air. The reed chair felt rough against his skin, and the metal contacts were gone. The Cartographer stood before him, her gloved hands stained with ink, several maps spread across the table. Her mask was still in place, but her shoulders were slumped, as if the weight of what she'd seen had settled there.

"You saw her," Dar said, his throat dry. "The woman in the garden."

The Cartographer nodded, picking up a quill and dipping it in ink. "She's the one who's been hunting my signal too. The one who wants the Lily to bloom." She began to draw, her movements precise but hurried. "The Chorus facilities—their clinics, reliquaries, tithe-routes. Everything I have."

Dar watched as the maps took shape, each line and symbol representing a piece of the Church's hidden network. The Cartographer worked quickly, her breath visible in the cold air of the Undergleam. "There," she said, pointing to a series of interconnected lines. "The main Choratorium here, the nursery there, and the reliquaries where they keep the harvested voices."

She pushed the maps across the table toward Dar. "Take them. But there's one more thing." She picked up a fresh sheet of parchment and began to draw again, her hand shaking slightly this time. A single shaft, deep beneath the Marrowworks, older than the Church above it. At its base, a single word: THRONE. And around the shaft, lines of old ledger-script: DO NOT COUNT THE HEARTBEATS.

Dar leaned closer, squinting at the script. "What does that mean?"

The Cartographer looked up, her eyes shadowed behind the brass mask. "Because it counts back," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "The heartbeats in that place aren't just alive—they're aware. And they remember."

Dar and Suri moved quickly through the Undergleam, the maps rolled tight in Dar's coat pocket. The Lily in his chest was quiet now, but he could feel it processing what it had shown the Cartographer, the dream of the salt gardens and the humming woman. "What do you think she meant by 'it counts back'?" he asked Suri as they navigated a tunnel that sloped upward.

"The Church has something old beneath the Marrowworks," Suri said, counting her steps. "Something that remembers being counted. Something that might be hunting us back."

They emerged from a maintenance shaft into the Saltstairs, the tenement warrens rising around them like teeth. The salt storm had passed, leaving behind a thick fog that blurred the edges of buildings and swallowed distant sounds. A warden's whistle cut through the fog, sharp and demanding. Suri grabbed Dar's arm, pulling him into an alley just as a grey-coated figure passed by, hand-cranked pulse-reader clicking rhythmically.

"They're checking for vital-debt defaulters," Suri whispered, pressing herself against the damp wall. "We need to move before they get to this sector."

Dar nodded, the weight of the maps heavy in his coat. The Lily pulsed once, a reminder of the debt it had placed on him—blood for growth, darkness for strength, and now this: a shared dream with a woman who seemed to be everywhere and nowhere at once. They had a lead, a path to the Church's secrets, but it was tangled with something older and far more dangerous. As they slipped back into the fog, Dar wondered if they were running toward answers or running straight into the thing that counted back.
